THE NICHOLSON STORY

Every firm has a founding story. Most of them are about a business plan. This one is about a trade learned by hand, a house that mattered, and a friendship that turned into a company almost by accident.

1991

A trade learned by hand, not by title

A trade learned by hand, not by title Bill Nicholson was still a student at Ohio State when he started learning the business the honest way, working alongside his older brother John at Nicholson Evans Construction. The real education came that same season, in his parents' own home in German Village.

Restoring it properly meant learning from a master finish carpenter named David Collins, and it was there, cutting joints and setting trim in a house that mattered to him personally, that Bill discovered the work wasn't just a job. It was the thing he was good at. It was also where he met Mike Fought, the aeronautical engineer who would eventually become his partner. Neither of them knew it yet.

After finishing his degree in construction technologies, Bill spent the next several years sharpening his craft on other people's projects, project managing for Dave Fox and running a division at J.S. Brown & Company, quietly building the kind of reputation that never shows up on a resume.
2003

A partnership lasting over 20+ years

By 2003, the pattern was hard to ignore. Bill's wife said it. The subcontractors he worked alongside said it. Clients said it, more than once: you're clearly passionate about this, why aren't you doing it for yourself? Eventually he stopped arguing with them and started his own remodeling company in Columbus.

Business found him almost immediately, more of it than one person could carry alone, so within a few months he called the one person he trusted with the parts of the business that weren't his strength: Mike Fought. Mike said yes. Bill brought the eye for construction detail and the instinct for taking care of people. Mike brought the engineering mind, the systems, the processes, the discipline that lets craftsmanship scale without losing itself.

Most partnerships don't last twenty years. Most don't survive a recession that closed firms twice their size, or the years since when the whole industry seemed to be relearning how to build. Most partners, sooner or later, decide they'd rather go it alone. What kept Bill and Mike at the same table wasn't luck. It took humility to admit when the other one was right, character to keep a promise when it would have been easier not to, and enough trust, in themselves and in each other, to keep building through every year that tried to talk them out of it. More than two decades later, that trust is still exactly why it works.
Bill Nicholson, Partner

today

Recognized for the craft, remembered for the relationship

Nicholson has grown into one of the most sought after design build firms in central Ohio, and the awards on the wall prove the craft holds up under scrutiny. But ask Bill or Mike what they're proudest of, and it's rarely the trophies. It's the families who call back for a second project, then a third, then send their kids' first home renovation our way too. Long standing relationships aren't a marketing line here. They're the whole business model, the same one two guys built on a handshake in 2003.

It was never a two person job, though. What started with Bill and Mike has been carried, year after year, by architects, designers, and craftsmen who treat a client's home the way you'd treat a neighbor's, with care that doesn't wear off. Some of the people who built this company are still walking its job sites today. Others have gone on to build wonderful careers of their own around the country, carrying a little bit of Nicholson with them wherever they landed. A few, we've had to say goodbye to along the way, and we carry them with us too.

We've watched this team's kids grow up alongside the company itself, some of them now working right beside the parents who raised them. The name on the door has only ever belonged to two people. The company has always belonged to everyone who showed up and did the work with the same care as if it were their own home.
